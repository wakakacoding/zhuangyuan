Zhuangyuan Lu (状元录)

A sourced database of the zhuangyuan (状元) — the first-place graduates of China's imperial civil examinations — from the Tang to the Qing dynasty. Each biographical claim cites a passage in a historical source, and what we could not verify is marked 待考证 ("to be verified") instead of guessed.

The site itself is in Chinese. This page summarises the project; names, quotations and most data tables stay in Chinese. Figures below are computed from the data at build time.

Scope

  • 666 civil zhuangyuan in the roll, covering exam years 622–1904 (military zhuangyuan are not included).
  • 209 have a full biography; the rest have a short "dossier card" with the evidence found so far.
  • 368 recorded relationships (kinship, teacher–student, recommendation, impeachment, rivalry), 216 anecdotes, 387 people with a hometown coordinate, and 112 catalogued sources.

How reliable is it?

Two separate questions are tracked.

  1. Was this person really first in his year? Each roll entry has a grade: A 256 — checked by hand against a standard history, an official exam register, or a contemporary epitaph / funerary biography / inscribed pass-list / Siku abstract that explicitly says first place; B 322 — a full-text search of the histories finds the name next to "first place" wording (biography not yet read in full), or the claim appears only in gazetteers, encyclopaedias, Ming–Qing notes or the Qing poetry anthology Wanqing yishi hui (these never rise above B); R 22 — the biography was read and does not say he was first (disputed); C 66 — listed only in a Chinese Wikipedia list so far.
  2. Does each cited quotation really occur in the source? A script compares every quotation with the locally held source text, ignoring punctuation and variant characters. Of 2067 quotation-bearing items (life events, anecdotes, relationships, roll evidence), 2024 pass — a verification rate of 97.9%. 26 are marked as inferred and 17 are marked "to be verified".

Anything containing 待考证 should not be cited as settled. See the data dictionary (Chinese) for field-by-field definitions, and the statistics page for the verification table.

Privacy

No third-party analytics, ads or tracking scripts, no cookies, and no visitor counting. Everything a page loads comes from this site. Favourites and display preferences stay in your own browser (localStorage).
